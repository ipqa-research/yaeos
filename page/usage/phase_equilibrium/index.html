Style guide

Imagine cooling a closed bottle containing methane and butane. Initially it may contain only gas; after cooling, a liquid can appear. Both substances can be present in both phases, but in different proportions.

  • A component is a chemical substance in the model (methane, butane, …).
  • A phase is a homogeneous portion of the system (a vapor, a liquid, or a second immiscible liquid). Two components do not imply two phases.
  • The feed composition describes the entire bottle, not either phase separately. A phase fraction tells you how much material is in a phase, whereas a phase composition tells you what that phase contains.

These routines predict equilibrium according to the selected model. They do not describe how fast droplets form, heat transfer, or the time required to reach equilibrium.

In yaeos, most phase-equilibrium procedures return the EquilibriumState type, which holds the phase compositions, molar volumes, pressure, temperature, phase fraction, and iteration information. Multiphase calculations return MPEquilibriumState.

Which calculation should I choose?

  • “How much liquid and vapor do I have at this T and P?” Use a flash.
  • “At what pressure does the first bubble/drop appear at this T?” Use saturation pressure. Fix pressure instead to find a saturation temperature. At a boundary the new phase has zero limiting amount; saturation does not determine a finite split inside the two-phase region.
  • “Where is the boundary over a range of T and P?” Trace an envelope from one checked saturation point. It follows a branch, not every possible boundary of a complicated mixture.
  • “Should this homogeneous feed split at all?” Start with stability analysis. A failed two-phase flash is not the answer to that question.
  • “Where do the two phases become identical?” Use critical calculations.
  • “Could two liquids coexist with vapor?” Read the multiphase page, including the current wrapper’s implementation limitations.

A volume root is not a complete equilibrium result

An equation of state can have several volumes at the same composition, temperature, and pressure. root_type="liquid" or "vapor" selects a root; "stable" selects the preferred homogeneous root at that composition. This does not test whether a lower-Gibbs-energy state exists at different phase compositions.

Likewise, equality of fugacities and material balance give a candidate equilibrium. A local solver can converge to identical phases, a metastable split, or an equilibrium that is unstable to a third phase. Global equilibrium requires the lowest appropriate thermodynamic potential among all admissible states. Stability searches help test this but finite numerical searches are not a proof that no other state exists.

General workflow

A typical phase-equilibrium workflow is:

  1. Build a model with the desired equation of state and mixing rule.
  2. Check the stability of the feed with min_tpd when the phase behaviour is uncertain.
  3. Calculate a flash at the conditions of interest.
  4. Use the resulting compositions as initial guesses for nearby points.
  5. For a complete phase diagram, start from a saturation point and continue it with the envelope routines.

Start with the complete PT flash example. Its material-balance and fugacity checks are useful even when the solver reports success.

Practical notes

  • Check the routine’s result contract, finite positive conditions, normalized nonnegative compositions, phase fractions, and component balance. Failure handling is not uniform; critical and multiphase routines need extra checks.
  • Use initial guesses (k0, p0, t0, y0) from previous calculations when scanning a region. Continuation is more robust than independent calculations.
  • Near critical points, compositions and volumes approach each other and phase fractions become poorly determined. Try continuation, smaller steps, and alternative initial guesses; changing solver does not remove this sensitivity.
  • For liquid-liquid equilibria, perform a stability analysis first and consider forcing the light phase to the liquid root in flash_second_order.
  • Activity-coefficient models (GeModel) require an initial K-factor estimate for flash; min_tpd can provide one. flash_second_order initializes these models with stability analysis when k0 is absent.
  • Validate model parameters against data in the temperature, pressure, and composition region of interest. A converged cubic-model water/hydrocarbon split, for example, is not by itself a validated prediction of that system.
