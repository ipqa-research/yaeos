# GERG2008 parameters

This page lists every parameter that `yaeos` uses to evaluate the
[GERG2008](gerg2008.html) residual Helmholtz energy: the pure-fluid
constants, the pure-fluid residual coefficients, the binary reducing
parameters and the binary departure functions. Use it to check what the model
assumes about a component or a pair, or to compare `yaeos` with another
implementation.

The tables were generated from the values that the compiled module
`yaeos__models_ar_multifluid_parameters_gerg2008`
(`src/models/residual_helmholtz/multifluid/parameters_gerg2008.f90`) holds
after it loads its tables, and were checked against the literals in that file.
They describe the code, so if the source changes, the source wins.

How to read the tables:

- **Indices.** \(i\) and \(j\) are the internal GERG2008 table indices, equal
  to the values of the `G2008Components` fields (`G2008Components%methane` is
  1, `G2008Components%argon` is 21). They are **not** the component order of
  a model built with [[gerg_2008]]: the constructor picks the rows and pairs
  that belong to the identifiers you pass.
- **Pair order.** Binary parameters are listed once per pair, with
  \(i \lt j\). The reverse order is derived, as explained in
  [Binary reducing parameters](#binary-reducing-parameters).
- **Residual part only.** `yaeos` implements only the residual contribution.
  The module also stores the ideal-gas coefficients (`n0i`, `th0i`) of the
  original formulation, but no `yaeos` routine uses them, so they are not
  listed here.
- **Precision marks (†).** Two coefficients are written in the source without
  a double-precision exponent, so the compiler stores them as single-precision
  reals. The tables show the literal from the source. The values `yaeos`
  actually uses are 0.11450634151697159 (ethane, \(k=7\)) and
  -0.7326539158821106 (generalized departure function, \(k=4\)), a relative
  difference of about \(10^{-8}\).

\(T_{c}\) and \(\rho_{c}\) are the critical temperature and critical density
of each pure-fluid equation. They reduce the pure-fluid equations and build the
mixture reducing functions \(T_r\) and \(V_r\) described on the
[GERG2008 page](gerg2008.html#composition-dependent-reducing-functions).
\(V_{c}=1/\rho_{c}\) is computed by `yaeos` (shown here rounded to six decimals)
and is what `model%components%vc` returns.

The other columns are not used by the GERG2008 Helmholtz energy:

- \(M\) is stored in the module but not used by any `yaeos` routine.
- \(P_{c}\) and \(\omega\) only build the Soave-Redlich-Kwong model that
  provides initial volumes for the volume solver, and they are what
  `model%components%pc` and `%w` return. The source gives \(P_{c}\) in Pa
  and `yaeos` converts it to bar. Most of these values are written as
  single-precision literals; the table shows them rounded back to the
  digits written in the source.

# Pure-fluid residual coefficients

The reduced residual Helmholtz energy of pure fluid \(i\) is

\[
\alpha^{o,i}_r(\delta,\tau)=
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\mathrm{pol}}} n_{k}\,\delta^{d_{k}}\tau^{t_{k}}
+\sum_{k=K_{\mathrm{pol}}+1}^{K_{\mathrm{pol}}+K_{\mathrm{exp}}}
n_{k}\,\delta^{d_{k}}\tau^{t_{k}}\exp\left(-\delta^{c_{k}}\right),
\]

where \(n_{k}\) are dimensionless coefficients, \(d_{k}\) and \(c_{k}\) are
integer density exponents and \(t_{k}\) are temperature exponents. In the mixture,
every pure-fluid term is evaluated at the **mixture** reduced density
\(\delta=\rho/\rho_r\) and reduced inverse temperature \(\tau=T_r/T\), not at the
pure-fluid critical values.

\(c_{k}\) applies only to the exponential terms, so it is shown as "–" for the
polynomial terms (\(k\le K_{\mathrm{pol}}\)). Many fluids share the same term counts and exponents \(d_{k}\), \(t_{k}\),
\(c_{k}\) and differ only in \(n_{k}\): methane, nitrogen and ethane (1, 2, 4)
share one set, and fluids 5 to 14, 16, 17, 19 and 21 share another. Carbon
dioxide, hydrogen, water and helium each have their own. Every fluid still
has its complete table below.

# Binary reducing parameters

These four parameters enter the reducing functions on the
[GERG2008 page](gerg2008.html#composition-dependent-reducing-functions), where
\(B^v_{ij}=\beta_{v,ij}\), \(G^v_{ij}=\gamma_{v,ij}\), \(B^t_{ij}=\beta_{T,ij}\)
and \(G^t_{ij}=\gamma_{T,ij}\). All are dimensionless.

The table lists each pair with \(i \lt j\). For the reverse order `yaeos` uses

\[
\beta_{v,ji}=1/\beta_{v,ij},\qquad \gamma_{v,ji}=\gamma_{v,ij},\qquad
\beta_{T,ji}=1/\beta_{T,ij},\qquad \gamma_{T,ji}=\gamma_{T,ij},
\]

so the reducing functions do not depend on the order in which you list the
components. A pair with all four values equal to 1 has no fitted reducing
parameters; its unlike interaction follows the combining rules in the
reducing functions alone.

# Binary departure functions

The departure function of pair \((i,j)\) is

\[
\alpha^{ij}_r(\delta,\tau)=
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\mathrm{pol},ij}} n_{k}\,\delta^{d_{k}}\tau^{t_{k}}
+\sum_{k=K_{\mathrm{pol},ij}+1}^{K_{\mathrm{pol},ij}+K_{\mathrm{exp},ij}}
n_{k}\,\delta^{d_{k}}\tau^{t_{k}}
\exp\left[-\eta_{k}\left(\delta-\varepsilon_{k}\right)^2
-\beta_{k}\left(\delta-\gamma_{k}\right)\right],
\]

and it enters the mixture energy multiplied by \(x_i x_j F_{ij}\). All
coefficients are dimensionless. Only the 15 pairs below have a departure
function; for every other pair \(F_{ij}=0\) and the term is skipped. Seven pairs
have a specific function. The other eight share one generalized function and
differ only in \(F_{ij}\).

| 1 | 4 | Methane + ethane | 1 | Specific |
| 1 | 5 | Methane + propane | 1 | Specific |
| 1 | 6 | Methane + n-butane | 1 | [Generalized](#generalized-departure-function) |
